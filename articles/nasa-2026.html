NASA in 2026: Missions, Achievements and What Comes Next

A structured look at human exploration, planetary science, astronomy and the missions shaping NASA's next phase.

Artemis and the return to lunar exploration

NASA's Artemis program is designed to develop a sustained human presence around and on the Moon while testing systems and operations that can support later exploration. Artemis missions combine crewed spacecraft, commercial landing systems, surface technology and international partnerships.

Europa Clipper

Europa Clipper is NASA's robotic mission to Jupiter's moon Europa. Its scientific goal is to determine whether Europa has conditions that could support life. The spacecraft will make repeated close flybys and use a suite of instruments to study the moon's ice shell, surface and environment.

Perseverance and Mars science

The Perseverance rover continues NASA's work at Mars by studying the ancient environment of Jezero Crater and collecting rock and soil samples. These samples are important to future plans for studying Martian material on Earth.

Webb, Hubble and the wider science portfolio

The James Webb Space Telescope and Hubble Space Telescope continue to investigate galaxies, stars, planets and the evolution of the universe. NASA's broader portfolio also includes Earth-observing satellites, heliophysics missions and robotic planetary exploration.

What comes next?

The next phase is less about one spectacular mission than about building a connected exploration architecture: spacecraft, habitats, science instruments, launch systems and commercial partners working across multiple destinations. Dates and mission configurations can change, so ORYNT treats NASA's official mission pages as the reference point for schedule updates.
