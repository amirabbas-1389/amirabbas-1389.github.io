NVIDIA: From Graphics to the AI Infrastructure Era

NVIDIA began with a focus on 3D graphics. Three decades later, its GPUs, software and networking technologies sit at the center of a much broader accelerated-computing ecosystem.

NVIDIA was founded in 1993 by Jensen Huang, Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem. The company’s early vision focused on 3D graphics for gaming and multimedia. NVIDIA’s corporate timeline identifies 1999 as the year it introduced the GPU concept, followed by CUDA in 2006, which opened GPU parallel processing to a wider range of scientific and research workloads.

The important shift: programmable acceleration

The significance of NVIDIA is not limited to graphics hardware. CUDA helped developers use GPUs for general-purpose parallel computing, creating a software ecosystem around accelerated computation.

From deep learning to modern AI systems

The modern AI wave made accelerated computing much more important. NVIDIA identifies the 2012 AlexNet breakthrough as a milestone in the company’s AI history. Since then, its platform has expanded beyond individual GPUs into systems combining compute, networking and software.

Why infrastructure matters

Large AI models require enormous amounts of computation and data movement. That makes the relationship between chips, memory, networking and software increasingly important. NVIDIA’s current strategy therefore includes systems and networking alongside its GPU architectures.

What comes next?

In 2026, NVIDIA is pushing its Vera Rubin platform toward large-scale AI infrastructure. The company has announced full-production plans and describes the platform as a foundation for large AI factories and advanced AI workloads.

Recent announcements also show NVIDIA expanding into physical AI, local AI, networking and quantum-computing software. These developments broaden the company’s story beyond graphics processors toward the systems required to operate accelerated computing at scale.
