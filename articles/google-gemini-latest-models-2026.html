Google Gemini in 2026: The Latest Models and Features

Google’s Gemini family is expanding beyond chat and text generation, with newer models focused on coding, agents, live audio, speech generation and real-time interaction.

Gemini 3.8 Flash: built for demanding work

Google made Gemini 3.8 Flash generally available on September 2, 2026. The company describes it as its most capable Flash model, designed for long-running software engineering, autonomous agents and complex enterprise workflows. It is aimed at tasks that may require planning, tool use and multiple steps rather than a single response.

For developers, the model is available through the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. Availability, usage limits and pricing can vary by product and account.

Gemini 3.8 Live and Live Extended Thinking

On September 15, Google announced general availability for two audio-to-audio models through its Live API:

  • Gemini 3.8 Live: designed for low-latency, real-time voice agents and natural dialogue, with tool calling and live session updates.
  • Gemini 3.8 Live Extended Thinking: adds more intensive background reasoning during audio conversations for cases where a voice agent needs to work through a more complex request.

These are developer-facing model options; their presence in the API does not necessarily mean every feature is available in the consumer Gemini app.

New speech generation models

Google’s September 22 API update made Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS generally available. Text-to-speech (TTS) systems turn written text into spoken audio. Google says the Flash TTS model targets expressive, high-fidelity speech and longer-form stability, while Flash-Lite TTS is designed for lower-latency and higher-throughput applications.

The same update introduced voice design and an expanded voice library, alongside voice replication features that Google says require consent verification.

Gemini 3.7 Flash remains part of the lineup

Introduced in August 2026, Gemini 3.7 Flash focuses on coding, web development, knowledge work and agent workflows. Google published benchmark comparisons against Gemini 3.6 Flash, but benchmark results depend on the test, prompt, configuration and evaluation method; they should not be treated as a guarantee for every real-world task.

What does this mean for everyday users?

For people using Gemini through Google’s consumer apps, the models and features they see depend on their region, subscription, device and staged rollout. Developer API announcements describe capabilities available to builders, not necessarily features immediately enabled for every user.

In broad terms, the newest announcements point toward three areas: stronger multi-step work and coding, more natural live voice interaction, and more expressive generated speech.
