Intel 2020–2026: From Manufacturing Reset to the 18A Era

How Intel moved from a major manufacturing and product reset toward foundry ambitions, 18A production and a new generation of AI PCs.

2020–2023: a company rebuilding its roadmap

Intel entered the 2020s facing pressure on process technology, product execution and manufacturing economics. The response increasingly centered on a broader manufacturing strategy: accelerate process development, expand capacity and build a foundry business that could manufacture chips for other customers.

2024: Intel Foundry becomes a central part of the strategy

In February 2024 Intel formally launched Intel Foundry as a systems foundry aimed at the AI era. The roadmap included advanced process nodes, packaging and a wider customer ecosystem. Intel also highlighted Intel 14A, 18A and specialized node evolutions as parts of its longer-term manufacturing plan.

18A: the key manufacturing milestone

Intel 18A combines RibbonFET gate-all-around transistors with PowerVia backside power delivery. Intel reported that 18A entered production in 2025. In June 2026, Intel said the enhanced 18A-P variant had entered risk production. These are important milestones, but Intel's performance and manufacturing claims should be read as company-reported results rather than independent benchmarks.

AI PCs and the Core Ultra Series 3

Intel's 2026 client strategy is strongly tied to AI PCs. Core Ultra Series 3 is built on Intel 18A and launched in 2026 across a large partner ecosystem. Intel positions the platform around CPU performance, integrated graphics, battery efficiency and local AI processing.

What comes next?

Intel's roadmap continues toward 14A and further process and packaging generations. The strategic question is no longer only whether Intel can make competitive processors, but whether it can execute a scalable manufacturing and foundry model while rebuilding customer trust and maintaining product competitiveness.
