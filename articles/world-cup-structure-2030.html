FIFA World Cup: Hosts, Champions and the Road to 2030

From Uruguay 1930 to Spain 2026: the countries that hosted the tournament, the teams that lifted the trophy, and why 2030 will be unlike any previous World Cup.

The FIFA World Cup is more than a month-long football tournament. It is a recurring global event whose structure has changed with the sport itself: the number of teams has expanded, hosting has moved between continents, and the tournament has repeatedly introduced new formats and technologies.

World Cup at a glance

The men's FIFA World Cup began in 1930. The tournament was not held in 1942 or 1946 because of the Second World War. From 1950 onward, it returned to a regular four-year cycle. The 2026 edition expanded the field to 48 teams, a major structural change from the 32-team format used from 1998 through 2022.

What changes with 48 teams?

The 2026 World Cup marks the first 48-team edition. That expansion changes the scale of qualification, group-stage scheduling and knockout progression. It also creates a larger global footprint, with matches spread across Canada, Mexico and the United States.

Why 2030 is different

The 2030 World Cup will be the centenary edition. Morocco, Portugal and Spain are the official tournament hosts. At the same time, Uruguay, Argentina and Paraguay will each stage one centenary celebration match. In total, six countries across three continents are connected to the 2030 edition.

FIFA says the three South American matches will be played a few days before the official opening matches in Morocco, Portugal and Spain. The schedule is designed to give the teams involved in those early matches additional time to travel, rest and prepare. FIFA's current plan places the opening matches in mid-June and the final on 21 July 2030.

What could make 2030 memorable?

  • A 100-year connection: Uruguay hosted the first World Cup in 1930, and Montevideo will be central to the centenary celebration.
  • Three continents: Europe, Africa and South America will all be part of the tournament footprint.
  • Travel becomes part of the story: FIFA has specifically designed additional rest and travel time around the South American centenary matches.
  • A mature 48-team era: 2030 will be the second World Cup after the expansion from 32 to 48 teams.

The bigger picture

World Cup history is also a history of how international football organizes itself. Hosts influence the tournament's geography and identity; format changes determine how teams reach the knockout stage; and champions reflect different football cultures and generations. The 2030 edition adds another layer: it is simultaneously a modern multi-country tournament and a celebration of where the competition began.
