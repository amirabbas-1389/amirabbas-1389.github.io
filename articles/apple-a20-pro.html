Apple A20 Pro: Inside Apple’s New 2nm iPhone Chip

Apple’s A20 Pro brings a 2-nanometer manufacturing process, higher memory bandwidth, a new CPU and GPU design, and a larger Neural Engine to the iPhone 18 Pro generation.

When Apple introduced the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max in September 2026, the company also introduced the A20 Pro, its latest high-end iPhone system-on-chip. The chip is built around a 2-nanometer process and is designed to handle demanding workloads ranging from gaming and photography to on-device artificial intelligence. Apple announced the chip on September 9, 2026, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro lineup. Apple Newsroom.

The move to 2nm

The manufacturing process is one of the most important changes in A20 Pro. Apple says the chip is built using the latest 2-nanometer process technology. Moving to a smaller process allows chip designers to fit more sophisticated circuitry into a limited area while working to balance performance, power consumption and heat.

For a smartphone, that balance matters. A processor is not useful simply because it can reach a high peak performance level; it also has to maintain performance during long gaming sessions, video processing, photography workloads and AI tasks without excessive heat or power use.

A six-core CPU with integrated Neural Accelerators

A20 Pro uses a new six-core CPU. Apple says the CPU includes integrated Neural Accelerators and is designed for demanding workloads as well as everyday tasks.

Apple reports that A20 Pro has 50 percent more memory bandwidth than A19 Pro. Higher memory bandwidth can help the processor move data between the compute units and memory more quickly, which is particularly relevant to graphics, large AI workloads and other data-intensive applications.

A seven-core GPU

The graphics processor has seven cores. Apple says the new GPU design is up to 40 percent faster than A19 Pro for graphics rendering.

The improvement is relevant to high-end mobile games, 3D applications, video processing and other workloads that depend heavily on GPU computation. Apple’s current iPhone Pro platform also supports hardware-accelerated graphics features such as ray tracing.

Neural Engine: 32 cores for on-device AI

Artificial intelligence is a central part of A20 Pro. Apple has equipped the chip with a Dual 16-core Neural Engine, giving it 32 Neural Engine cores in total.

Apple says the new Neural Engine provides double the AI processing power of A19 Pro. The purpose is not simply to make benchmarks larger. The additional AI capability is intended for workloads such as on-device AI models and computational photography.

This matters because more AI processing can happen directly on the phone. Apple's iPhone 18 Pro generation combines the chip with Apple Intelligence and the new Siri AI features in iOS 27, although feature availability can vary by language and region.

Thermal design is part of the story

A20 Pro is also connected to a redesigned thermal architecture. Apple says the chip uses custom packaging inspired by its M-series silicon, with the silicon die positioned alongside the memory so that the chip can connect directly to a new vapor chamber.

The new vapor chamber has three times the surface area of the previous generation in iPhone 17 Pro, according to Apple. Apple says the combined design enables up to a 40 percent gain in sustained performance compared with the previous generation.

The distinction between peak and sustained performance is important: sustained performance describes how well a device can continue operating under a demanding workload over time, rather than how fast it performs for only a short burst.

A20 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro

The A20 Pro is used in the iPhone 18 Pro and iPhone 18 Pro Max. Apple pairs it with other custom silicon, including the N1 wireless networking chip and the C2 cellular modem system.

N1 enables Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6 and Thread. Apple says C2 brings AI-powered improvements to cellular quality and reliability and consumes 15 percent less energy than C1X while providing faster uploads. These components show how modern smartphone performance increasingly depends on several specialized chips working together rather than on the application processor alone.

What the A20 Pro changes for users

  • Gaming: the seven-core GPU targets higher graphics performance and demanding 3D workloads.
  • AI: the larger Neural Engine is designed for more capable on-device AI processing.
  • Photography: the chip supports computational photography workloads alongside the new camera system.
  • Long workloads: the new thermal architecture is designed to help maintain performance for longer periods.
  • Efficiency: the 2nm process and redesigned silicon are part of Apple’s effort to balance performance and power consumption.

Why A20 Pro matters beyond one iPhone

The significance of A20 Pro is broader than a single product cycle. Smartphone processors are increasingly being designed around several different kinds of computing at once: general-purpose CPU work, graphics, machine learning, image processing, connectivity and power management.

The move to 2nm also illustrates how semiconductor manufacturing remains central to the evolution of consumer electronics. As transistor dimensions become smaller and chip designs become more specialized, manufacturing technology, packaging and thermal engineering become as important to the final experience as raw CPU speed.

Conclusion

A20 Pro represents Apple’s latest push toward smaller process technology and specialized mobile computing. Its 2nm process, six-core CPU, seven-core GPU, 32-core Neural Engine, higher memory bandwidth and redesigned thermal architecture are all aimed at the same challenge: delivering more computing capability while keeping a smartphone practical to use.

The most important change may therefore not be a single specification. It is the way Apple is combining silicon, memory, cooling, AI acceleration and software into one platform for the iPhone 18 Pro generation.
