The Organizations That Shape Our World

A guide to six major international institutions—and the different roles they play in diplomacy, regional integration, energy, policing and development finance.

United Nations (UN)

The United Nations was founded in 1945 and currently has 193 Member States. Its system includes the General Assembly, Security Council, Economic and Social Council, International Court of Justice and other bodies. The UN provides a forum for diplomacy and international cooperation across peace and security, development, humanitarian affairs and international law.

European Union (EU)

The European Union is a political and economic union of 27 European countries. It operates a single market and has common institutions that make and administer EU-level rules in areas covered by the treaties. The euro is used by 21 EU countries according to the EU's current facts and figures.

African Union (AU)

The African Union is a continental organization consisting of 55 Member States. It was officially launched in 2002 as the successor to the Organisation of African Unity. Its work covers continental cooperation, peace and security, integration, development and representation of African interests.

OPEC

The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries coordinates petroleum policies among its members and publishes extensive data about global oil markets. OPEC currently lists 12 Member Countries. It should be distinguished from the broader OPEC+ cooperation framework, which includes participating non-OPEC producers.

INTERPOL

INTERPOL is an international police-cooperation organization, not a single global police force. It currently has 196 member countries. National Central Bureaus connect national police authorities to the INTERPOL network so they can share information and cooperate on cross-border investigations.

World Bank Group

The World Bank Group consists of five institutions: IBRD, IDA, IFC, MIGA and ICSID. IBRD and IDA together form what is commonly called the World Bank. The Group provides financing, guarantees, private-sector support, technical expertise and international investment-dispute services for development-related work.

The key point is that these institutions are not interchangeable. Some are political or regional unions, some coordinate a specific sector, some facilitate international cooperation, and some provide financial or legal mechanisms. Understanding their different mandates makes international news easier to interpret.
