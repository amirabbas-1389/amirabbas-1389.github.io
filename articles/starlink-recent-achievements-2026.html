Starlink’s Recent Milestones: More Satellites, Direct-to-Cell and Global Reach

From frequent Falcon 9 launches to partnerships that aim to bring satellite connectivity directly to ordinary mobile phones, Starlink’s recent developments show how low-Earth-orbit communications are expanding beyond home broadband.

1. A rapid launch cadence keeps growing the constellation

On September 19, 2026, SpaceX launched 27 Starlink V2 Mini Optimized satellites on a Falcon 9 rocket from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. The mission, designated Starlink 15-27, was reported as SpaceX’s 81st Starlink mission of the year. Its first-stage booster completed its 17th flight and landed on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You. Spaceflight Now reported the satellites deployed a little over an hour after liftoff.

Reusable boosters are central to this launch rhythm: returning and refurbishing the first stage allows SpaceX to fly the same hardware repeatedly rather than build a new booster for every mission.

2. Direct-to-Cell is moving from messaging toward broader mobile service

Starlink’s Direct-to-Cell system is designed to connect compatible mobile phones to satellites when terrestrial cellular coverage is unavailable. Starlink’s 2025 progress report said the first-generation constellation had been deployed with more than 650 satellites launched over 18 months, and that the service had connected more than 12 million people at least once. These are company-reported cumulative figures, not a count of people continuously connected at the same time.

Current Direct-to-Cell availability and features depend on country, mobile operator, device and regulatory authorization. In Ukraine, the national communications regulator reported in June 2026 that Kyivstar had begun testing a Light Data stage, expanding beyond SMS to selected messaging and navigation applications.

3. Japan’s KDDI signs up for next-generation Starlink Mobile V2

On September 15, Japan’s KDDI announced an agreement with SpaceX for next-generation Starlink Mobile V2 satellites. KDDI says it aims to offer high-speed broadband and native voice calls on mobile phones in areas beyond terrestrial network coverage by the end of 2027. The planned service builds on the existing au Starlink Direct partnership.

KDDI also describes use cases such as emergency communications, international access, IoT connectivity and support for infrastructure resilience. The 2027 date is a stated target, not a confirmation that nationwide service is already available.

4. Starlink’s reach extends beyond residential broadband

Starlink has also become a connectivity option for aviation, maritime travel, businesses and public-sector organizations. In its 2025 progress report, the company reported more than 9 million customers across over 155 countries and markets, as well as service to more than 21 million airline passengers and 20 million cruise passengers during the reporting period.

These company-reported totals illustrate the range of use cases, although service performance can vary with location, network demand, equipment, weather and local rules.

What comes next?

The recent milestones point to three parallel areas of development: continued satellite deployment, improving direct-to-phone connectivity, and partnerships with mobile operators. The next-generation Mobile V2 plans are particularly notable because they aim to bring more broadband-like capability and voice calls to standard mobile devices. Their rollout will depend on satellite deployment, compatible spectrum, operator integration and regulatory approvals.
