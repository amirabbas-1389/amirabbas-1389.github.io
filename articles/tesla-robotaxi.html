Tesla Robotaxi: The Rise of Autonomous Ride-Hailing

Tesla is expanding a driverless ride-hailing service in several U.S. cities, combining its autonomous-driving technology with a dedicated Robotaxi app and a growing fleet.

What is Tesla Robotaxi?

Tesla Robotaxi is the company's autonomous ride-hailing service. Instead of a conventional taxi driver, the vehicle handles the trip using Tesla's automated-driving technology. Riders request a vehicle through the Robotaxi app, enter a destination, review the estimated fare and wait time, and start the ride after the vehicle arrives.

As of September 2026, Tesla says Robotaxi service is operating in limited areas of Austin, Dallas and Houston, Texas, and Miami, Orlando and Tampa, Florida. Availability depends on the local service area and operating hours.

Which vehicles are being used?

The current fleet includes modified Model Y vehicles and the new Cybercab. Tesla says the Model Y Robotaxi can carry up to four passengers, while Cybercab is designed for up to two passengers.

Cybercab is particularly notable because Tesla designed it without a steering wheel or pedals. Tesla describes it as a fully autonomous vehicle with two-seat, bench-style seating, a central touchscreen and dedicated space for luggage.

How does a Robotaxi ride work?

  1. Open the Robotaxi app and enter a destination within the available service area.
  2. Review the estimated fare and wait time, then confirm the ride.
  3. Use the app to locate the arriving vehicle and verify its license plate.
  4. Enter the vehicle, fasten the seatbelt and tap “Start Ride.”
  5. During the trip, passengers can adjust features such as climate settings and use the vehicle's touchscreen.

Tesla also provides a “Pull Over” function through the app or touchscreen. According to Tesla, the vehicle can stop at the nearest safe location and connect the passenger with customer support.

The technology behind the service

Tesla's autonomous-driving approach relies heavily on camera-based perception and neural-network processing. Tesla's published FSD safety material says its vehicles use eight external cameras to provide a 360-degree view and process large amounts of visual information in real time.

It is important to distinguish Tesla's consumer FSD (Supervised) product from Robotaxi operation. Tesla states that FSD (Supervised) requires active driver supervision and does not make a privately owned vehicle autonomous. Robotaxi is a separate service in which Tesla operates vehicles in designated service areas without a conventional driver performing the driving task.

Cybercab and the future of Tesla's fleet

Tesla has said that Cybercab is intended to become a major part of the Robotaxi fleet. In its 2026 first-quarter update, the company said Cybercab is expected to begin replacing the existing Model Y fleet once it enters production and could become the highest-volume vehicle in the Robotaxi fleet over time.

Tesla has also been preparing additional U.S. markets. Its first-quarter update listed Phoenix and Las Vegas among markets in preparation, alongside Florida markets that were then being prepared for service. Actual availability can change as testing, permits and deployment progress.

Regulation and the road to wider deployment

Autonomous ride-hailing is not only a software problem. Companies must also meet transportation, safety and regulatory requirements in each market. Tesla's expansion therefore depends on local testing, permits and the rules governing automated vehicles.

Regulatory decisions can change quickly. For example, in September 2026 the Czech Republic provisionally approved Tesla's FSD system after earlier concerns involving issues such as speed-limit compliance and driver oversight. This concerns FSD approval rather than the U.S. Robotaxi service itself, but it illustrates the regulatory scrutiny surrounding Tesla's autonomous-driving technology.

What could change with Robotaxi?

If autonomous ride-hailing expands, the technology could change how some people use cars. A vehicle could potentially spend more time providing rides instead of remaining parked, while passengers could use travel time without personally driving.

At the same time, the long-term impact depends on factors including safety performance, regulation, service coverage, vehicle production, pricing and public adoption. Tesla's current service remains geographically limited, so the experience in today's Robotaxi markets should not be treated as proof of what autonomous transportation will look like everywhere.
